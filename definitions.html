1. Project targets

Target full torque [Nm]

The maximum positive driving torque that should remain available at the target full-torque speed.

Default input: 10 Nm. The model deliberately caps available torque at this target; it does not show the absolute physical maximum of the motor.

← Back to calculator

Target self-rotation speed [rpm]

The speed the wheel should reach under FFB torque, for example during self-alignment or countersteer in drifting. Default input: 300 rpm.

This is a loaded operating requirement, not a no-load speed. The specified minimum self-rotation torque must still be available at this speed.

← Back to calculator

Target full-torque speed [rpm]

The speed at which the full target torque should still be available. I use 10 Nm at 200 rpm, because I regard 200 rpm as the maximum wheel speed I can physically reach during rapid hand movements.

This is an input target for the torque–speed characteristic. It is distinct from the calculated full-torque corner speed, which depends on supply voltage, motor parameters and current limits.

← Back to calculator

Minimum torque at target self-rotation speed [Nm]

The minimum positive driving torque required at the target self-rotation speed. I use 2 Nm at 300 rpm: this provides a small margin above the torque needed to turn my wheel with its attached button box and no additional load.

The required torque depends on wheel inertia, friction and desired acceleration. The calculator does not solve the wheel acceleration dynamics.

2. Motor measurements

Torque constant Kt [Nm/A]

Kt relates torque to torque-producing current:

T = Kt · Iq
T
Motor torque [Nm]
Kt
Torque constant in the current-amplitude convention [Nm/A]
Iq
Torque-producing current amplitude [A]

The default input of 0.56 Nm/A was experimentally determined for the project motor. It refers to the current-amplitude convention used by the model. Do not use an RMS-based constant without converting its current convention.

Torque was measured with a digital torque wrench in Peak mode. Two phase currents were read from the MKS XDrive Mini through ST-Link, and the third was inferred from Kirchhoff’s current law. A peak torque measurement does not establish a continuous torque rating.

← Back to calculator

Line-to-line resistance [Ω]

Measure between two motor phase wires. This field expects line-to-line resistance, not one winding’s resistance to the internal star point.

Disconnect the motor from the controller. Measure each of the three wire pairs with the motor stationary and at a known temperature. Results should be similar. Significant differences can indicate a damaged motor, but first rule out measurement error, poor contacts or faulty connections.

The project used a four-wire measurement at 1 A to reduce the effect of leads and contacts. Default input: 0.35 Ω.

For a symmetrical star-connected motor, line-to-line resistance is twice one phase winding’s resistance. The model formulas already include this conversion: enter the measurement without dividing it by two. Other winding connections or current conventions require the model to be checked.

← Back to calculator

Winding temperature rise [°C]

Default input: 50°C. The project assumes a resistance measurement at 25°C and an intended maximum magnet operating temperature of 75°C. The difference, 75 − 25 = 50°C, is used as the temperature-rise assumption.

The calculator applies that rise to winding resistance:

Rhot = Rmeasured · (1 + 0.00393 · ΔT)
Rhot
Temperature-adjusted line-to-line winding resistance [Ω]
Rmeasured
Line-to-line resistance at the measurement temperature [Ω]
ΔT
Winding temperature rise relative to the measurement temperature [°C]
0.00393
Copper temperature coefficient used in the resistance correction [1/°C]

For 0.35 Ω and a 50°C rise, the calculated resistance is 0.418775 Ω.

The 75°C limit is the project owner’s assumption, not a universal magnet rating. Winding and magnet temperatures can differ. This correction does not predict either temperature, establish a safe duty cycle or verify magnet temperature.

3. Controller parameters

Current at 100% Power Limit [A]

The controller current represented by 100% FFBeast Power Limit in your hardware and firmware configuration.

The project uses 37 A with Amplifier Sense Gain 80 V/V. If a different gain is used, enter the corresponding current scale. Verify the scaling for your controller.

This value is the denominator in Required Power Limit and caps the modeled current. It is not a calculated operating current or a continuous thermal rating.

← Back to calculator

PWM voltage utilization [%]

This factor converts selected DC supply voltage to the voltage budget used by the loaded SVPWM model:

Ueff = Usupply · PWM
Ueff
Available motor voltage budget in the model convention [V]
Usupply
Selected DC supply voltage [V]
PWM
Voltage utilization factor, entered as percent but used as a fraction (85% = 0.85) [dimensionless]

For example, 85% utilization with a 36 V supply gives a calculated budget of 30.6 V. This is voltage utilization, not controller energy efficiency or a commanded fixed PWM duty cycle.

← Back to calculator

Power-stage efficiency [%]

Default input: 95%. Motor electrical input, approximated as mechanical output plus copper losses, is divided by this efficiency to estimate power-stage input. This is a design assumption, not an efficiency measurement at every operating point.

Torque vs. speed

Vertical guides use available torque at both input speeds, including target torque and controller current caps. Their callouts show that torque and its required voltage. If voltage limits positive torque, a point meets the supply line. If another cap applies first, it lies below the line. The green and blue rectangles are visual bounds below selected voltage and left of the torque guides, not complete feasible regions. A feasible point must also be on or above its speed curve and respect current and torque caps. The supply is assessed at its horizontal voltage line. 9. Abbreviations and conventions

FOC: field-oriented control. SVPWM: space-vector pulse-width modulation. PWM: pulse-width modulation. rpm: revolutions per minute. RMS: root mean square. DC: direct current. Power Limit: percentage of the configured controller maximum current.

The factors 0.75, 0.866 and 1.155 follow the assumed current and voltage definitions. Iq is current amplitude in the chosen FOC convention; resistance is measured line-to-line. The model omits inductance and controller dynamics. PWM denotes voltage utilization, not energy efficiency.

Six-step no-load speed is a separate estimate. The power result is not a global maximum across all speeds. These curves do not describe regenerative braking. Stationary-wheel torque is capped at the project target and controller limit and is not a continuous thermal rating.
